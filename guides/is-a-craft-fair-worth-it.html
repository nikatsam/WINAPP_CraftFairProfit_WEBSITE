Is a craft fair worth it for you?

There is no universal yes or no. A useful decision weighs the costs, the time, your goals and the uncertainty of a particular event.

Reviewed 15 October 2026 · This guide is not investment or business advice.

Start with the costs you can name

Write down the pitch, travel, parking, event supplies and any other direct cost. Keep the product cost of what you expect to sell separate. Estimate payment fees only as an assumption until you have the actual statement.

Estimate contribution, not just takings

Expected sales are not profit. Subtract the unit costs of the products you think may sell and any estimated processing fees from planned gross sales. The amount left is the contribution available to cover the pitch and other event costs. Then ask what sales mix would be required to cover those costs.

Include your time and capacity

Consider setup, travel, selling, packing down and recovery time. Can you prepare enough stock without sacrificing another important commitment? Is the event date, audience and stall format a fit for the products you want to show? These are personal choices that a calculator cannot answer.

Use a small experiment

If the fee is manageable and the risk is acceptable, a single event can teach you about display, questions customers ask, product mix and the organiser’s process. Decide ahead of time what you will observe. A quiet day is not automatically proof that a product or business cannot work; one busy day is not proof of reliable future income.

Illustrative scenario, not a testimonial or result claim: Rowan makes small-batch ceramics and considers a new indoor market. They compare the pitch and train fare with a conservative range of possible units, note which pieces have the strongest contribution, and decide how many they can replenish. Afterward they record actual sales, refunds, fees and returned stock so the next decision uses more than memory.

What about a boot sale or clearing clutter?

If you are taking handmade items or craft overstock to a local boot sale, a product-and-event record can help you keep a simple tally of stock and money. FairProfit is not a general household decluttering tool, classified site, auction platform or second-hand marketplace. Consider whether a simpler handwritten list is enough for a one-off sale.

Questions before you book

  • What is the total event-specific cost, including travel and parking?
  • How much stock can I bring and replenish comfortably?
  • Which product costs or fees might I otherwise forget?
  • What will I record on the day and after it?
  • What outcome would make attending worthwhile to me besides immediate profit?
